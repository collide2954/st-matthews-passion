# The work: plan and material

This document describes the plan of the new *St Matthew Passion* for one alto voice and one baroque orchestra.
The style study ([`style-study.md`](style-study.md)) gives the measured values and the sources that the plan uses.
The plan is in three files of the directory [`design`](../design): [`form.toml`](../design/form.toml), [`text.toml`](../design/text.toml), and [`pitch.toml`](../design/pitch.toml).

The music is not complete. The section "Status" at the end of this document lists the numbers that are written.

## The idea

The *St Matthew Passion* of Bach has two choirs, two orchestras, and a group of soloists.
Its text has three layers: the Gospel story, the poems of Picander, and the chorales.

The new work has one voice. The voice is the witness.
In the text that Bach set, a woman pours water on the head of Jesus in Bethany, women "watch from far away" at the cross, and two women sit at the tomb.
In this work one alto is that woman:

- She tells the story: she has the words of the Evangelist.
- She says the words of the other persons (Jesus, Peter, Pilate).
- She answers with the poems and with the chorales, as one person and not as a congregation.

The orchestra is all that the voice is not: the second choir, the crowd, and the congregation.
It has their music with no words. The score shows these words above the staves.

The work keeps the words of Bach's text: it selects sentences and lines, and it does not change a word.
The work is primarily in the musical language of Bach. Its form and its counterpoint use methods of later music.

## Four threads through the work

### The call

The smallest idea of the work is one leap up and then steps down.
Three themes of Bach have this shape.
The leap gets wider from number to number until the cry at the death.
After the death the leaps are small again.

| Leap | Where |
| --- | --- |
| A fourth | The first two notes of the Passion chorale (number 6) |
| A minor sixth | "Erbarme dich" (number 9) |
| A minor seventh | "Erbarm es Gott" (number 10) |
| An octave | The cry of Jesus (number 13). Bach has a major sixth here. |
| A semitone | The note B that goes to C above the last chord (number 16) |

The study measured the alto lines of Bach: a leap of a sixth or more is only 2 to 6 percent of the moves.
Thus each wide leap of the voice is an event.

### The chorale that comes apart

The Passion chorale ("Herzlich tut mich verlangen") comes four times, and each time it has less.

| Number | Key | Form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6 | E-flat major | Complete and plain: the voice and all instruments, with the setting of Bach |
| 8 | D major | A memory: two phrases of the winds, with no words |
| 11 | G minor to B-flat major | Complete, but bare: the voice, one viola da gamba, and the bass |
| 14 | A minor, ending on E major | Complete: the four voices of Bach, but not at one speed. The chords come apart, and they are complete again at the end of each line. The last line is the plain setting of Bach. The end is on the dominant, as in the setting of Bach. |

Bach has the chorale five times: in E major, E-flat major, D major, D minor, and A minor.
The tune is a tune for sopranos. In E major it goes to F-sharp 5, and in D minor to G5.
The alto of Bach has E5 as its highest note (table M4 of the style study).
Thus the first setting of this work is in E-flat major, the key of Bach for the words "Ich will hier bei dir stehen": there the tune goes to F5 one time.
The third setting is one fifth lower than the setting of Bach.

### Sharps and flats

The work goes from the keys with sharps to the keys with flats, as the work of Bach.
The two fugues of the crowd end in B major and in C-sharp major.
Immediately after them, the music of the scourge starts in F major.
From there the work stays in the flat keys to the end.

### The pulse

Three movements of Bach are in 12/8: the first chorus, "Erbarme dich", and "Mache dich, mein Herze, rein".
In this work the pulse of three eighth notes is a thread:
all twelve eighth notes of the bar (number 1), three plucked notes on each bass step (number 9), single plucked notes of a bell (number 12), and at the end one note in a bar and then no pulse (number 16).

### Number 14 in detail

The verse has eight lines. In each line, the table gives the speed of each voice of Bach: the length of its notes in relation to the notes of the voice.
The tune and the bass are always together: they are the frame of the harmony of Bach.

| Line | Words | Tune one octave higher (first violins) | Alto of Bach (second violins) | Tenor of Bach (violas) | Moments with a semitone clash |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Wenn ich einmal soll scheiden, | 2 | 1 | 1 | 0.19 |
| 2 | so scheide nicht von mir, | 1 | 1.5 | 1 | 0.17 |
| 3 | wenn ich den Tod soll leiden, | 2 | 1 | 1.5 | 0.22 |
| 4 | so tritt du dann herfür! | 1 | 1.5 | 1.5 | 0.29 |
| 5 | Wenn mir am allerbängsten | 3 | 2 | 1.5 | 0.29 |
| 6 | wird um das Herze sein, | 2 | 1.5 | 1.5 | 0.16 |
| 7 | so reiß mich aus den Ängsten | 1 | 1 | 1.5 | 0.00 |
| 8 | kraft deiner Angst und Pein! | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0.00 |

The first line says "when I must go away": the first violins have the tune at half speed, and they go away from the voice.
The second line says "do not go away from me": the first violins stay with the voice.
In line 5 ("when my heart is most afraid") each voice has its own speed.
The last line is the setting of Bach with no change.

The code tried all sets of speeds for each line and counted the moments with a semitone clash (the module `compose/ears.py`).
The sets of the table make an arch: the clashes increase to lines 4 and 5, and the last two lines have none.
For comparison: in the measured music of Bach, 7 percent of the chords of a plain chorale and 13 to 28 percent of the chords of an aria have a semitone clash (tables M1 and M6 of the style study).
